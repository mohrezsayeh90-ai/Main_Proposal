\begin{latin}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

This thesis studies particle-motion prediction and design across several flow and actuation models.

A parametric physics-informed neural network predicts particle trajectories from release conditions and elapsed time in prescribed Poiseuille flow. The predicted terminal event type agreed with independent DOP853 integration for all 70 test cases. The learned trajectory map was used to design multiparticle release schedules, which were evaluated by independent replay and synchronized pairwise separation.

Deep operator networks predict the flow field and magnetically driven motion.
Predictions were compared with finite-element references or independent integration.
Two published benchmarks were used to assess dedicated neural particle solvers.
The inverse flow studies reconstructed an inlet profile from timed trajectories or path geometry.
The inverse magnetic study designed admissible excitation to reproduce a timed target trajectory.
Performance was assessed by independent replay of the designed excitation.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Physics-informed neural networks, particle motion, release scheduling, operator learning, flow reconstruction, magnetic excitation

\end{latin}
